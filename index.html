Code

CLAP-AD separates path imitation from speed selection. It predicts a driving path, evaluates candidate speeds against safety criteria, and selects a speed that follows the user's request whenever it is safe.

Abstract

End-to-end autonomous driving is commonly trained by imitating a single expert behavior per scenario. This makes it difficult to learn alternative valid behaviors and leaves models vulnerable to noisy or ambiguous speed labels. We introduce Closed-Loop Aware Planning for Autonomous Driving (CLAP-AD), a plug-in planning head that separates path prediction from speed selection: it learns the path through imitation, then scores candidate speeds using multiple safety criteria grounded in physical constraints. At test time, a user can specify a desired speed, which the planner follows when feasible while falling back to safe alternatives as needed—without retraining. On Bench2Drive-Speed, CLAP-AD achieves a Speed-Adherence score of 82.53, exceeding the strongest baseline by 12.9 points. On Bench2Drive it improves the driving scores of UniAD and VAD by 28.5 and 32.7 points, respectively, while retaining strong performance with TFv6. On NAVSIM v2, it improves its LTFv6 backbone's EPDMS by 6.1.

How the Planner Works

CLAP-AD first predicts a feasible path from the scene representation provided by an existing end-to-end driving model. Dedicated scoring heads then evaluate candidate speeds along that path for collision risk, traffic-light compliance, and stop-sign compliance. At deployment, speed classes that pass the safety threshold are feasible; a selector can choose the feasible speed closest to a requested target, or use another policy such as the median for cautious driving. This planning head integrates with different backbones, including UniAD, VAD, and TFv6.

The path planner and criterion-specific speed-scoring heads work together to choose a safe, controllable speed.
